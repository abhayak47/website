Leave and attendance management: why they belong together

Leave and attendance describe the same thing from two sides: who is working and who is not. When they live in separate places, someone reconciles them by hand every month.

Guide · 5 min read · Published by the Onjaal team · Updated 15 October 2026

Download: Leave policy template (PDF)Free. A fill-in leave policy covering leave types, accrual, approvals and records.

The reconciliation problem

A manager approves a day off on chat. HR records it in a leave sheet. The biometric export still shows the person as absent. At month end, someone matches the three.

Every manual step is a place for an error, an argument or a delay.

What should flow automatically

  • An approved leave should show as leave in attendance, not as absence
  • Holidays and weekly offs should be applied once, from one calendar
  • Leave balances should update the moment a request is decided
  • Regularised punches should correct attendance and not need a second entry

Policies to align first

  • Leave types and how each is earned
  • Grace periods and what counts as a late mark
  • How and by when a missed punch can be regularised
  • Who approves, and who covers when the approver is away
  • How half-days, short days and comp-offs are counted

Signs you have outgrown manual tracking

  • Month-end closing takes days, not hours
  • Employees ask HR for their leave balance
  • Approvals are lost between inboxes
  • Managers keep their own side sheets
  • You cannot answer “who is out next Friday” in under a minute

Numbers worth watching

  • Attendance rate by team and by day of the week
  • Approval turnaround time
  • Late marks and regularisation requests
  • Leave taken against entitlement, so balances do not pile up unnoticed

How Onjaal approaches it

In Onjaal HRMS, approving a leave request updates attendance for those days, so managers and HR see the same picture. Employees can follow their requests, and approvals do not depend on a chat thread.

Worked example: one request, end to end

Rohan Deshmukh asks for casual leave on Friday. His manager approves it the same day. In a connected system the leave balance drops by one day, Friday shows as leave in attendance (not as absence), and the monthly report counts it correctly. In a patchwork of chat, a leave sheet and a biometric export, someone has to make those three changes by hand, and any of them can be missed. This example uses fictional people.
