Why Onjaal exists

Most growing businesses run on a patchwork.

Leave requests arrive on WhatsApp. Attendance lives in a spreadsheet or a biometric export. Employee details sit in a folder, and the approvals live in someone’s inbox. Every tool works, and none of them agree with each other.

We are building Onjaal so people, customers, operations and accounts can share one picture of the business. We start with the part every team has on day one: its people.

Before

  • Leave on chat, attendance on sheets
  • Approvals lost between inboxes
  • Month-end spent reconciling
  • Costs unclear until a sales call

With Onjaal

  • Leave flows into attendance
  • Requests easy to follow and decide
  • One record for every employee
  • Pricing published upfront
What we believe

Five principles we build and sell by.

They shape what goes into the product, and how we talk to you about it.

01

Practical by design

Start with the everyday problems teams actually have: a leave request, a missed punch, a form that needs a signature. Fix those well before adding anything clever.

02

Clarity over clutter

A screen should make the next step obvious. We would rather ship fewer, better-organised features than a long menu nobody reads.

03

Honest by default

Prices are published. Products in development are labelled as such, with no promised dates. Anything that depends on your setup is confirmed in writing first.

04

Connected from day one

Every product is designed to share people, customers and numbers with the others, so you enter a detail once and it is available where it is needed.

05

Built with intent

We grow thoughtfully, guided by what real teams tell us. If a request does not make work simpler for most customers, we will say so.

Our promise

Less scattered admin. More connected work. Told plainly, priced openly.

Built for Indian teams

Indian workplaces have their own rhythm: several leave types, regional holiday calendars, shift patterns, biometric attendance devices and managers who approve on the go. Onjaal HRMS is shaped around those realities, and priced in rupees.
